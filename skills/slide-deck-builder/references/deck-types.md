# Deck types and default outlines

Starting outlines for the four deck types a B2B service company builds most. Each line is one
slide: `layout · what the headline must say`. Cut slides the source cannot support; never pad.

## 1. Sales / pitch deck (10 to 14 slides)

Audience: a prospect who has not bought yet. Job: earn the next step.

1. title · the outcome the prospect gets, in their terms
2. bullets · the problem as the prospect described it (quote their words where possible)
3. stat · what the problem costs them now (only numbers they gave or public sources)
4. bullets · why the usual fixes fail (hiring, tools, doing nothing)
5. two_col · what changes with you: today vs after
6. table · how the engagement runs: phases, what they hold at the end of each
7. stat or quote · proof: one case with numbers, or a client quote with a name and role
8. bullets · what you need from their team
9. table · options and price ranges, if the source has them
10. closing · the ask: one decision, one date

## 2. QBR or board deck (8 to 12 slides)

Audience: an existing client's decision maker, or a board. Job: show what the quarter bought
and get decisions for the next one. Content comes from a finished narrative (see `qbr-builder`
if installed); this skill only lays it out.

1. title · the quarter in one sentence
2. bullets · main point: what worked, what slipped, the one move for next quarter
3. table · plan vs actual with a "% of plan" column
4. bullets · 3 to 5 events and what each changed in the client's numbers
5. bullets · what did not work, one cause, one conclusion
6. table · next quarter: item, owner, date
7. two_col · what we need from you / what we will deliver
8. closing · decisions requested, with deadlines

## 3. Case study deck (6 to 9 slides)

Audience: prospects similar to the featured client. Job: make the result believable.

1. title · the result, with its number and timeframe
2. bullets · who the client is (type and context, named only with permission)
3. bullets · the situation before, in the client's words
4. table · what was done, phase by phase
5. stat · results with sources and dates
6. quote · the client's own words
7. closing · "if this sounds like your situation" and the next step

If `case-study-writer` is installed, write the story there first and lay it out here.

## 4. Onboarding / training deck (8 to 15 slides)

Audience: a new client team or new hires. Job: people can do the first task without asking.

1. title · what they will be able to do after this session
2. bullets · who does what (roles, one owner per item)
3. table · the first 30 days: week, what happens, who
4. bullets · tools and access needed, and by when
5. two_col · do / do not, from real mistakes
6. table · where to find things (doc, link label, owner)
7. bullets · how progress is reported
8. closing · the first task and its deadline

## Headline test

Read only the headlines in order. They should tell the whole story without the bodies. A
headline that names a topic ("Market overview", "Our approach") fails; rewrite it as the
sentence the slide proves ("Mid-size firms lose one bid a month to slow quotes").
